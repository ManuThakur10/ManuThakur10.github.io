Part A

Exploring a pretrained flow model

01

Training Paths
& Sampling

Flow matching learns a time-dependent velocity field between Gaussian noise at t = 0 and clean images at t = 1. I used one photograph of poker chips throughout the denoising experiments so that every method saw the same image-noise pair.

1.1

Noisy training inputs

Linear interpolation scales both endpoints. Reusing a single sampled noise tensor isolates the effect of time: low t is almost pure noise, while the original composition becomes increasingly legible.

xt = (1 - t)ε + tx1

Endpoint checks recover ε at time 0 and the clean image at time 1.

1.2

Classical Gaussian denoising

Before blurring, I divide by t to restore the image amplitude. The best MSE-selected blur becomes weaker as more clean signal survives: σ = 8, 2, and 1 respectively. Blur suppresses noise, but at low t it also removes nearly all structure.

1.3

One-step denoising

A single model prediction estimates the remaining displacement to the clean endpoint. It restores semantic structure even at t = 0.25, where Gaussian blur cannot, though fine details are invented or softened.

x̂1 = xt + (1 - t)vθ(xt, t, c)

The model predicts velocity, so the update is added rather than subtracted.

1.4

Visualizing velocity

I compare the predicted update with the known update x1 - xt. Both use one shared symmetric RGB scale; the heatmaps show per-pixel RGB RMS error. At lower times, the model makes larger semantic corrections and the error concentrates around chip boundaries and printed details.

1.5

Iterative Euler denoising

Euler integration recomputes the velocity after every step, following the curved learned trajectory instead of extrapolating once. Starting at t = 0.50, 25 updates remove noise progressively. The final sample is sharper than the Gaussian baseline and follows the learned image manifold more faithfully than a fixed blur.

One step0.01743
Euler0.02186
1.6

Generating from noise

Starting at pure Gaussian noise and integrating 50 times produces complete images for the generic prompt a high quality photo. All five seeds converge toward portraits, revealing a strong subject bias; several faces also contain visible structural artifacts.

02

Classifier-Free
Guidance

Classifier-free guidance compares conditional and empty-prompt velocities at the same image and time, then pushes farther in the direction that distinguishes the prompt. I used the same five initial noises as the unguided samples for a direct comparison.

vcfg = vc + w(vc - vu)

With w = 7, the portraits become cleaner, more centered, and more photographically consistent, although their compositions are less diverse.

03

Creative
Applications

The same sampler becomes an editor when it begins partway along a path between a source image and noise. Small starting times give the model more freedom; large starting times preserve the source. Every sweep below uses t = 0.02, 0.04, 0.06, 0.10, 0.14, 0.25, 0.50.

3.1

Image-to-image editing

With the generic photo prompt, the Campanile progresses from a smooth vertical object to a detailed tower. The poker stacks and house show the same transition from semantic reinterpretation to reconstruction. Simple drawings return to their exact geometry unusually early because their large flat regions survive the forward process clearly.

3.2

Text-guided editing

Replacing the generic condition with a targeted prompt changes what the sampler treats as the nearest plausible image. The prompt dominates at low starting times; source layout, color, and fine detail progressively take control as t rises.

04

Orientation
& Scale

Instead of asking one condition to control the whole velocity field, I combine multiple predictions. Spatial transformations create images that change under rotation; frequency decomposition creates images that change with viewing distance.

3.3

Visual anagrams

One velocity is predicted upright. A second is predicted after a 180-degree rotation, rotated back, and averaged with the first. The dark painterly pair reads most strongly: the upright head becomes a bright fire surrounded by figures after rotation.

v = [va(x) + F(vb(F(x)))] / 2

F rotates both spatial axes by 180 degrees.

3.4

Hybrid images

At every Euler step, I take low frequencies from the distant prompt's velocity and high frequencies from the close prompt's velocity. A Gaussian low-pass with σ = 16 and kernel size 265 separates the two bands. The full-size image emphasizes texture; the reduced view exposes the global silhouette.

v = LP(vdistant) + vclose - LP(vclose)

Both predictions use the same image, time, CFG weight, and Euler step.

Part B

Training flow models from scratch

05

Learning to
Denoise

Part B replaces the pretrained image model with a UNet trained directly on MNIST. The first objective is deliberately simple: map a noisy observation back to its clean digit in one step. This establishes the architecture and exposes why one-step regression struggles as a generative model.

B.1.1

UNet architecture

The network uses convolutional blocks at 28, 14, and 7 pixels, a 7-to-1 spatial bottleneck, learned upsampling, and skip connections at matching resolutions. For denoising I used 128 hidden channels and an unconstrained single-channel output so the loss, rather than an output clamp, determines the reconstruction.

B.1.2

The noising process

I added independent Gaussian noise to the same ten test digits at seven standard deviations. At σ = 0.5, the training setting, digit identity remains visible beneath dense high-frequency corruption; by σ = 1.0, several strokes are difficult to distinguish.

z = x + σε,   ε ∼ N(0, I)

Pixels are normalized to [0, 1]; display clipping is used only for visualization.

B.1.2.1

Training the one-step denoiser

Training used fresh σ = 0.5 noise for every batch, Adam at 1e-4, batch size 256, and five epochs. The preview MSE fell from 0.01239 after epoch 1 to 0.00924 after epoch 5, and the later model restores smooth, recognizable strokes across all ten digits.

Training noiseσ = 0.5
Epoch 1 MSE0.01239
Epoch 5 MSE0.00924
B.1.2.2

Out-of-distribution noise

The denoiser is most faithful near its training level. With less noise, it slightly smooths already-clean strokes; with more noise, the outputs remain digit-like but progressively lose identity and inherit features from the model's learned prior.

B.1.2.3

Denoising pure noise

When the input is independent of its target, there is no evidence for which digit to reconstruct. By epoch 5, the network therefore produces nearly identical blurry digit-shaped images rather than distinct samples.

Interpretation

The outputs closely resemble the mean MNIST image. Because the noise is independent of the randomly paired target, the conditional distribution of the target given the input is just the full data distribution. Under squared error, the risk-minimizing prediction is its mean, explaining the shared soft, superimposed appearance instead of ten distinct digits.

06

Flow Matching
From Scratch

One-step regression averages ambiguous targets. Flow matching instead learns a velocity at every point along a continuous path from Gaussian noise to data, allowing many small, state-dependent updates to construct a sample.

xt = (1 - t)x0 + tx1

The target velocity is constant along each training pair: u(xt, t) = x1 - x0. At sampling time, Euler integration repeatedly reevaluates the learned field.

B.2.1 - B.2.3

Time-conditioned generation

Two small fully connected blocks embed continuous time and modulate the bottleneck and first decoder stage. I trained the 64-channel UNet for ten epochs with Adam at 1e-2 and exponential decay, then sampled with 50 Euler steps. The mean epoch loss fell from 0.17793 to 0.09321.

Hidden channels64
Euler steps50
Final mean loss0.09321
Observed progression

Digit-like strokes already emerge after one epoch. By epochs 5 and 10, most samples are recognizable and cleaner, though this unconditional model still produces ambiguous hybrids and uneven class coverage because it must represent every digit with time as its only conditioning signal.

B.2.4 - B.2.6

Class conditioning and guidance

A one-hot class vector modulates the same decoder stages as time. During training, class conditioning is dropped for 10% of samples. During generation, conditional and unconditional velocities are combined with guidance scale 5.0 and integrated for 300 steps. Class control makes the requested columns immediately visible and improves convergence.

v = vu + 5(vc - vu)

Each column requests digits 0 through 9; each row uses a different initial noise.

Class dropout10%
Guidance scale5.0
Final mean loss0.08468
B.2.6 · Scheduler ablation

Can the scheduler go away?

I trained a fresh model with constant Adam learning rate 1e-3 instead of beginning at 1e-2 and decaying by one order of magnitude over ten epochs. The seed, data order, model, batch size, conditioning dropout, and sampling settings remain fixed.

Scheduled start1e-2
Constant rate1e-3
Constant final loss0.08490
Result

Lowering the fixed learning rate preserved comparable epoch-10 performance. Both grids are legible and class-correct, with similar stroke quality and variation. The smaller constant step compensates for removing decay by avoiding the overly aggressive late-training updates that a fixed 1e-2 rate would produce.

07

What the
Velocity Carries

The most surprising part of the project was seeing the same mathematical object from both sides. With PixNerd, I manipulated a rich pretrained velocity field; with MNIST, I watched one emerge from noisy data. In both cases, generation depends on repeatedly asking what direction makes sense from the current state, time, and condition.

Time is an edit dial

Early starts favor the model prior; late starts preserve source geometry and detail.

Guidance trades diversity

CFG improves coherence and prompt alignment while narrowing the visual outcomes.

Integration matters

Recomputing velocity follows the learned path instead of assuming one straight extrapolation.

Perception is conditional

Orientation and spatial frequency can partition one image into multiple readings.

Ambiguity becomes an average

Without informative input or iterative structure, squared-error regression collapses many possible targets into their mean.

Conditioning organizes the field

Class labels turn an uneven unconditional generator into ten controllable, rapidly converging modes.
