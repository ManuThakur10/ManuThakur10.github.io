01

Convolution
from Scratch

I implemented same-size convolution twice using only NumPy: first with four loops over the image and kernel, then with two loops and a vectorized patch operation. Both versions flip the kernel and use zero padding, including the asymmetric padding needed by even-sized derivative filters.

Tests against scipy.signal.convolve2d matched to floating-point precision: the largest measured error was 3.553 × 10⁻¹⁵.

Four-loop core 
for row in range(H):
    for col in range(W):
        total = 0.0
        for ky in range(kh):
            for kx in range(kw):
                total += (
                    padded[row + ky, col + kx]
                    * flipped_kernel[ky, kx]
                )
        output[row, col] = total
Two-loop core 
for row in range(H):
    for col in range(W):
        patch = padded[
            row:row + kh,
            col:col + kw
        ]
        output[row, col] = np.sum(
            patch * flipped_kernel
        )
02

Edges, Noise
& the DoG

Finite differences estimate the horizontal and vertical intensity derivatives. Combining them yields an orientation-independent edge strength; I selected a threshold of 0.18 to retain the cameraman and tripod while suppressing most background texture.

‖∇I‖₂ = √(Iₓ² + Iᵧ²)

The gradient magnitude combines signed x and y responses into one nonnegative edge image.

Smoothing before differentiation

(I ∗ G) ∗ Dₓ = I ∗ (G ∗ Dₓ)

Associativity turns blur-then-differentiate into one convolution. The measured interior differences were approximately 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁵ in both directions.

03

Unsharp
Masking

A Gaussian isolates the low frequencies. Subtracting that result from the original produces a high-frequency residual, which is scaled and added back to increase local contrast around edges.

Isharp = I + α(I − G ∗ I)

Larger α strengthens edges, but excessive values amplify noise and halos. The two-step and single-filter implementations agreed within 3.37 × 10⁻¹⁴.

04

Hybrid
Images

Hybrid images exploit scale-dependent perception. Fine edges dominate nearby, while distance removes those high frequencies and reveals the broad low-frequency structure underneath.

Each pair is aligned with left-eye, right-eye, and nose landmarks using a similarity transform. Warps use constant zero fill; a warped validity mask then crops both aligned sources to their common valid overlap so padding never enters the filters.

H = Gσₗ(A) + β[B − Gσₕ(B)]

The first term supplies global form. The second supplies fine structure; β controls how strongly that nearby interpretation appears.

Derek + Nutmeg

The required starter pair uses Derek for low frequencies and Nutmeg for high frequencies. Eye landmarks receive extra weight to stabilize the perceptual alignment after rotation.

Brindle dog + tabby

Similar frontal geometry and warm brindle coloring make this the most controlled custom experiment. Its final eye-eye-nose residuals are 6.29, 6.21, and 7.07 pixels on the aligned crop.

Tiger + lion

The tiger supplies low-frequency structure, while the lion contributes close-view fur and facial edges. This pair changes interpretation most clearly when the page is viewed at different zoom levels.

05

Gaussian &
Laplacian Stacks

Unlike a pyramid, every stack level keeps the original dimensions. Repeated Gaussian smoothing removes progressively broader frequency content; adjacent differences isolate the removed bands.

The final Gaussian image becomes the lowest-frequency Laplacian residual. Summing all six Laplacian levels reconstructs the original with a maximum numerical error of 2.22 × 10⁻¹⁶.

Lᵢ = Gᵢ − Gᵢ₊₁

The decomposition telescopes: I = L₀ + L₁ + ··· + LN−1.

06

Multiresolution
Blending

A hard cut is visible at every frequency. Multiresolution blending instead combines corresponding Laplacian bands using a Gaussian stack of the mask. Fine bands transition locally; coarse color and illumination change over a much wider region.

The full RGB arrays pass through the stacks, and the same mask level is broadcast across all three channels, so color is blended automatically.

Lᵢblend = MᵢLᵢᴬ + (1 − Mᵢ)Lᵢᴮ

Collapsing the blended bands produces the final image without a single scale carrying the entire seam.

Vertical mask

The Oraple

Seven levels, a narrow 18-pixel feather, σ=5 image stacks, and a smoother σ=10 mask stack preserve the apple and orange textures while removing the hard center seam.

Desert + ocean

Both horizons are placed at row 300 on a 600×900 canvas. A seam-aware ocean crop avoids the rocks; a mild row-wise tone match and a 170-pixel vertical feather create a gradual atmospheric transition.

Moon + coffee

The moon is resized and translated into a 720×720 crop of the cup. An ellipse centered at (360, 330), with radii (153, 143), follows the coffee opening; its Gaussian mask stack softens the circular boundary.

Inside the blend

Masked Laplacian levels

Each row shows the moon band, coffee band, and their masked sum. The early rows carry fine rim and crater detail; later rows carry broad illumination and color. The final residual closes the reconstruction.

07

What
Worked

Alignment first

Filter tuning cannot rescue mismatched facial geometry. Eye–eye–nose landmarks and valid-overlap cropping mattered more than tiny σ changes.

Sources matter

Similar pose, brightness, and color make hybrid perception more coherent. The brindle dog and tabby pair improved when the source palette improved.

Frequency is scale

High frequencies communicate edges and texture nearby; low frequencies communicate identity, lighting, and silhouette from farther away.

Masks are multiscale

A single feather is not enough. Blurring the mask separately at every stack level is what makes both fine detail and broad color transition well.
